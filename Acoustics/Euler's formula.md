# Euler's formula

$$e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$$

**Simplified reading:** a real exponent grows or shrinks, an imaginary exponent rotates. Raising $e$ to $j\theta$ walks a point around the unit circle by the angle $\theta$ with no changes to amplitude

**At $\theta = 0$ the result is $1$, purely real:** the imaginary component exists but sits at zero until an angle is introduced. As $\theta$ grows the point lifts off the real axis, the imaginary component $\sin\theta$ appearing at the expense of the real component $\cos\theta$, the two always trading so the radius stays $1$.
