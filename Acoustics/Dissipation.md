- **Setup**: the damper force is known directly, and dissipation is that force's rate of work.

$$F = r\,v, \qquad P = F\,v = r\,v^2$$

- **Real cycles**: for energy we drop to the real cos form. Energy quantities are quadratic, and products break the phasor machine, so the complex e is for linear work only.

$$\xi = X\sin\omega t \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \omega X\cos\omega t$$

- **Rate**: substitute in. Velocity enters twice (once in the force, once in the work rate), so cos ends up squared and the amplitude picks up omega squared.

$$P = r\,\omega^2 X^2\cos^2\omega t$$

- **Average over the cycle**: symmetry argument, cos squared and sin squared share one average and sum to 1 everywhere, so each averages a half. Average rate is half the coefficient of the cyclic term.

$$\bar P = \tfrac{1}{2}\,r\,\omega^2 X^2$$

- **Per cycle**: multiply by the cycle clock and the word rate retires, rate of work becomes work by specifying time.

$$E_d = \bar P\cdot\frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \pi\,r\,\omega\,X^2$$
